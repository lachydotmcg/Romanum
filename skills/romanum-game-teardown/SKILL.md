# Roblox game teardown

Answer one design question through observed play. A teardown explains what a player can do and what to test in another game; it does not prove why a competitor succeeds.

## Choose and inspect

Identify the experience by universe/place ID and record the date, version if known, device and evidence source. Find candidates through `research_game_idea`, chart data or the developer's references. Those tools locate games; they do not inspect gameplay. Use authorised play, recorded footage or supplied observations to examine mechanics. If access is missing, return the inspection questions without inventing answers.

Inspect enough of the first session to describe the fantasy, first action, feedback, next choice, reward, progression and recovery from a mistake. Note what makes another player's activity visible and whether it supports cooperation, competition or learning. Record specific moments, such as a waypoint hidden by a wall, rather than labels like "bad onboarding." A short session cannot establish the quality of the endgame.

For an intended younger audience, test comprehension, reading load, touch controls and fair recovery. Ask what the player understood and chose to do. Do not substitute the adult reviewer's preferences for the audience or assume a colour, theme or random reward appeals to every child.

## Keep evidence and interpretation separate

Use a small record:

| Observation | Design hypothesis | Next check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What happened, with a timestamp or source | Why it might help or hinder this audience | What evidence could support or contradict it |

Public player counts can describe popularity at a recorded time. They cannot reveal tutorial completion, revenue, audience ages or the cause of growth. Compare only genuine recorded history; do not draw a trajectory through missing samples. Ads, featuring, updates, weekends and traffic mix can change the outcome independently of the inspected mechanic.

## Learn from a prediction

Write the hypothesis before the follow-up, including what would change your mind. Choose an observation window and comparable metrics appropriate to the question. Keep unsuccessful predictions in the record and investigate counterexamples; selecting only eventual hits teaches a misleading lesson. A correct popularity prediction still does not isolate its cause.

A future check is a plan until an available scheduler has actually created it. Otherwise leave a dated follow-up task. Never claim to have watched a game or revisited it without observations.

Recommend one transferable design principle and a small original prototype. Test a meaningful player decision, interaction or clearer explanation. Do not copy distinctive assets, branding or a whole experience. Keep the report as short as the question allows.
